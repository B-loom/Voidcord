What's new

v1.2.9 — Accounts, settings and language

Reloading no longer signs you out

  • Ctrl+R sent you back to the account picker. On reload, Discord opens the gateway and identifies itself before Nebula has had a chance to step in. The user account was then taken for a bot when READY arrived, and the client signed itself out. Nebula now recognises the account as soon as the page loads, without waiting for the identify.

The language says what it does

  • Changing the language looked like it did nothing: Nebula's texts are read once, at load time, and screens already on display kept the old language. The choice was being saved; what was missing was saying so. A banner now announces it and offers to reload — which no longer signs you out. Settings that touch the network (DNS, proxy, cache) say instead that they will wait for the next launch.

Settings, inside the page

  • The documentation and the changelog exist in English. Choosing English in the settings only changed buttons and labels; those two pages stayed in French, and they are the longest ones. They are translated, and they follow the language.
  • Nebula's settings are shown and changed in the Nebula tab, under "Settings": the language, the startup screen style, automatic update checks, encrypted DNS, the system proxy. The separate window is still there, but you no longer have to go through it. Every change is written straight away.

What Nebula was holding back without knowing

  • Favourites and server order came back empty, even after the route was fixed: the plugin was replacing Discord's network call with a read from its local database, the one that only serves bots. The fixed route was therefore never reached. A user account now goes back out to the network.
  • Custom stickers were refused for everyone. The refusal came from a hard-coded "return false", fair enough for a bot, applied to accounts as well. Same story for long messages: Discord now decides for itself, account by account.

Worth knowing about Nitro

Discord has three subscriptions, and **Nitro Basic does not open the same doors as full

Nitro**: it keeps custom stickers to their home server and stops uploads at 50 MB instead

of 500. Nebula no longer misrepresents a user account's subscription: what the client

announces is now yours. Those caps are lifted on the client side — Discord's servers remain

free to refuse.

Your account was no longer quite yours

  • Real Nitro was being downgraded. Nebula announced "Nitro Classic" for *every* account, yours included: Discord was therefore refusing stickers to someone who has full Nitro. That invention is meant for a bot, which has neither a subscription nor a phone; a user account now keeps its own.
  • Purchases shown as owned came from the same place: the shop believed you already had decorations you did not have.
  • Your favourite GIFs, stickers and emoji came back empty, and **your server order was lost on the first launch.** Same cause: both of those live in your Discord settings, and Nebula was answering in their place from its local database — the one it keeps for bots, which have no settings at Discord. A user account is now sent back to Discord.

Language

  • Choosing "Français" on the welcome screen gave an English client. Discord applied the account's language over Nebula's once signed in. Nebula now imposes its own.
  • A "Nebula settings" button in the Nebula tab opens the settings window — language, startup screen, automatic updates, DNS. It could only be reached through the system tray icon.

Profiles

  • A game you do not know was showing on your profile. Discord's catalogue maps games to executable names, and several games claim the same ones — "game.exe", "launcher.exe". Nebula kept whichever came first, so a game at random. Shared names are now set aside: better to say nothing than to say anything.
  • The signed-in bot wears every badge, like the system account.
  • The system account was born with Nebula. Its profile said 13 May 2015, the date Discord was created, because it borrowed an id from that era. It now shows 29 October 2026, the day the first version was published.

The shop and blocked windows

  • The "Add a payment method" window span forever. It now shows the usual explanation, like the other pages that need Discord's payment machinery.

Signing back into an account

  • The "Sign in" button in the account picker really does sign you back in. Until now it sent you to the "Add an account" screen, then to Discord's own form: Discord looks for the token in *its* store, which does not hold Nebula's, concludes there is none and shows "Try signing in again". Nebula now takes over that click and signs in with the saved token, as if it had been pasted by hand.
  • No more "Windows Security" window asking for a passkey. It came from Discord's login form and led nowhere, since the local server already refuses WebAuthn. The request is blocked before anything opens.
  • Ctrl + R no longer signs you out. A session opened properly survives a reload.

Accounts and profiles

  • A bot no longer shows the game running on your machine. Game detection shows what is running on the computer: on a bot that is not its activity but yours, and it has no business on its profile.
  • Discord's settings cache is emptied when switching accounts: it is not filed per account, and the next one inherited the previous one's settings.

Interface

  • The colours are right again. Discord removed nine colour variables (--text-normal, --header-primary, --background-primary…): Nebula's screens that named them ended up with wrong shades, or a background that no longer matched the client's. Thirty-one places were reworked.
  • The shop says so instead of spinning. The "Add a payment method" window kept loading forever: it needs Discord's payment machinery, which is not here. It now shows the usual explanation.
  • The empty band above the account bar: the quest card was hidden but its box remained, and a box keeps its height. The hiding no longer looks for a precise class name — Discord renames them with every release — but walks up to the element placed in the sidebar.

Updates

  • A progress bar appears over the client, wherever you are, and says when the download is done. It only existed in the Nebula tab: you had to think of it and open it to know where the update stood.

Diagnostics

  • The log finally receives what Nebula writes from inside the client. It only kept Discord's errors: Nebula's own messages — signing back into an account, the gateway closing, share quality — never reached it, even though those are exactly what you go looking for in %APPDATA%\Nebula\logs\main.log.
  • Settings and favourites were out of reach from an extra client window: the answer was only handed back to the main window.

v1.2.8 — The base moves to DiscordBotClient 3.10.3

The whole core of the application was taken over from version 3.10.3 of the original

project, with everything it brings, and Nebula's additions were carried onto it one by one.

New in 3.10.3

  • A startup screen, in two styles: modern, or Discord's way. It also says what happened when startup fails, with a button to try again.
  • A real settings window, with a search box, replacing the configuration editor where the file had to be written by hand. It is in French.
  • Encrypted DNS accepts several providers (Cloudflare, Google, Quad9), and the system proxy can now be followed instead of direct connections.
  • The update check at startup can be turned off.
  • The upload limit goes up to 20 MB.
  • The old embed editor is back, to be enabled in the BotClient plugin's settings if you prefer it to the message editor.
  • Application emoji appear in the emoji picker.
  • From "New message", you can open a direct message with someone from their id.

Fixed in 3.10.3

  • The bot's token is checked before certain internal requests.
  • Missing slash commands are back.
  • A message over 2,000 characters no longer breaks sending.
  • Forum and Media channels no longer stay stuck on their loading skeleton.
  • Loading someone's profile no longer breaks when application data is missing.
  • An error in an internal exchange (IPC) no longer freezes the application.
  • Several window-handling faults, and the local server hardened after security reports.
  • Protocols are handled faster, and needless round trips between the client and the main process are gone.

On Nebula's side

  • The extra client windows (one per account) were reattached to the main process's new access control: without that they were no longer allowed to talk to it, and a second window's title bar acted on the first.
  • Encrypted DNS stays on Cloudflare by default, as before.
  • Discord is pinned to stable build 657850, Vencord to 1.15.15. Vencord removed some older components there: Nebula's screens were adapted.
  • A new startup image, and the line drawing that precedes it was redone from it. The credit button disappeared with the old illustration: Nebula no longer claims an author for the image shown.

Fixed right after

  • Signing in with a Discord account was refused with "unauthorised IPC sender". The new access control in 3.10.3 only allowed the separate login window, while Nebula has drawn the add-account screen inside Discord since 1.2.7. Both are accepted, and only those two.
  • The add-account screen's text was unreadable, black on a dark background. Discord renamed its colours in build 657850: --text-normal, --header-primary and --header-secondary no longer exist, and a colour that cannot be found is not applied at all. Every colour borrowed from Discord now has a fallback. The screen-share screen, the token login screen and the Nebula tab had the same fault.
  • Error messages that had come back in English are translated again: the IPC sender, an invalid bot token, the MessageContent intent warning, configuration errors and the confirmation for deleting data. The add-account screen no longer shows Electron's wrapping ("Error invoking remote method…") around the message.
  • A bot that tries to share its screen is finally told. The button did nothing: Discord reserves "Go Live" for user accounts, and the bot gateway does not carry those operations. A message now says so instead of silence.
  • Screen sharing would not start at all, even with a user account. The hardening in 3.10.3 only accepted "media" requests with sound and without video — but a screen-share request arrives with an empty list of types. It was therefore refused in a millisecond, before Nebula's picker even existed. The camera is still refused. That decision now lives in a function of its own, and a test pins down all eight cases: it can no longer change in silence.
  • A 12 MB file was refused with "Max file size is 1.00 GB", which meant nothing. Discord raised its default limit to 20 MiB on 3 September 2026; 3.10.3 only raised the Nitro tiers, leaving 10 MiB for everyone else — so for every bot. The floor is back up to 20 MiB. Beyond that you need a boosted server: 50 MB at tier 2, 100 MB at tier 3.
  • The red block behind the microphone button, when muted, is gone. The crossed-out icon was enough.

Screen sharing

  • Quality and frame rate can be chosen in the picker, as you share: 720p, 1080p, 1440p or the source's own resolution, at 15, 30 or 60 frames per second. The choice is kept from one share to the next. It is a ceiling placed on the capture: Discord re-encodes for voice afterwards, and may go below it.

Other fixes

  • An invite opened from the client still showed the "Discord App Detected" page, which offers to open a desktop application — a dead end, since Nebula *is* the application. The detour was only taken for links opened in a new window; it is now taken when the link opens in place as well.
  • Every gateway close is now written to the log with its code and reason, in %APPDATA%\Nebula\logs\main.log. A disconnection "for no reason" will leave something to understand it by.
  • The last two login error messages still in English are translated.

New plugin

  • NebulaPlateforme picks the device you appear to be connecting from: computer, browser, Android phone, iPhone, console or VR headset. That is the little badge others see next to your name. The change takes effect on the next connection.

v1.2.7 — Screen sharing, built-in windows and tidying up

New

  • Screen sharing works. It never had: no capture handler was installed, and Electron refused the request without a word. A picker now opens inside Discord, with Screens and Windows tabs, live thumbnails and the choice to include the computer's sound.
  • Signing in happens inside Discord, in a client window, instead of a separate one. The captcha keeps its own: hCaptcha is only accepted on a page served by Discord.
  • A NebulaStatut plugin keeps your activity off your profile — the game you are playing, or everything else if you want.
  • Plugins written for Nebula have their own section at the top of the plugins page, separate from Vencord's.
  • The welcome message gains a button to the project's website.

Fixes

  • Screen sharing never started, even once capture was in place: Nebula was cutting the "streamCreate", "streamWatch", "streamPing" and "streamDelete" gateway operations for everyone, when only a bot cannot use them. The stream was therefore never created. "callConnect" had the same fault.
  • Vencord's two screen-sharing plugins, written for browsers, got in the way: one replaced the capture and demanded 4K at 120 frames per second, the other wrote 80 Mbit/s into the negotiation. They no longer apply here.
  • An invite opened from the client showed the "Discord App Detected" page. It now opens directly inside Discord.
  • In the login window, typed text was unreadable on a dark theme and the buttons were misplaced.
  • Discord's account picker filled the screen with no way to scroll.
  • The bot token login screen had stayed in English, as had the screen-share settings window.

Tidying up

  • The "Boost your profile" card, the quests panel and the activity line under your name no longer show: none of the three leads anywhere here.
  • The "Send a gift" window shows the usual explanation instead of the Nitro offer.

v1.2.6 — Joining a server, and updates

Fixes

  • A user account can finally join a server. Nebula refused every invite with "cannot join guilds": that refusal came from the original plugin, written for bots, and did not look at the account type. Server templates had the same fault.

New

  • The Nebula tab says whether the application is up to date, with a green or red dot, and a button starts the check.
  • Downloading an update shows its progress, then a button to restart and install it.

v1.2.5 — Server link

Fixes

  • The welcome message's button led to an expired invite.
  • It now copies the link instead of opening it: the browser does not know your account and so could not get you into the server. A window confirms the copy, and the link is to be pasted into Discord.

v1.2.4 — First published version

New

  • Nebula warns you when a new version is published. The check now targets this project's repository, no longer the original one, whose releases would have replaced Nebula with a different application.

Changes

  • The project installs through a real Windows installer, and the code is published on GitHub.

v1.2.3 — Buttons in the welcome message

Changes

  • The plain-text server link gives way to two buttons: join the Discord server, and open the project on GitHub, each with its logo.

v1.2.2 — Tidying up and the login window

Fixes

  • The login window no longer cut off the top of the list when there were many accounts, leaving the form off screen. The list now scrolls on its own.

Changes

  • Discohook's build, its copy in the resources and the Android sources were removed from the project: about 200 MB that served no purpose any more.
  • Code written for Vencord now lives in nebula/, under version control, and is copied onto the clone at every build. A Vencord update no longer erases it.

v1.2.1 — Quests as well

Changes

  • Quests are blocked on both clients: their progress is checked by Discord's official client, so they would never advance here, account or bot.

v1.1.9 — Blocking case by case

Changes

  • Only Nitro is blocked on both clients, since neither a bot nor Nebula can handle a subscription. The Shop, Quests and Discovery are only blocked for a bot: a Discord account belongs there.
  • The panel finds the sidebar's edge better, which changes from page to page.

v1.1.8 — The message replaces the page

Changes

  • Nitro, the Shop, Quests and Discovery show the message in place of the page, full size, instead of sending you back to the home screen. The sidebar stays usable.

v1.1.7 — French and blocked pages

New

  • Vencord's plugins are described in French, and the page listing them too.
  • NebulaApparence allows the display name and the username to be changed separately.

Fixes

  • The shop, Nitro and quests are finally blocked, as is discovery for a bot. The filter placed on Discord's router could not see the sidebar's links; it now watches the address itself.
  • The author card for Nebula's plugins showed the profile of a Vencord contributor, whose id had been picked up by mistake. It now shows Nebula.

v1.1.6 — Documentation, appearance and blocked pages

New

  • A "NebulaApparence" plugin allows the display name to be changed and badges to be picked. Everything is local: nothing is sent to Discord and nobody else sees it.
  • Plugins written for Nebula carry its logo in the plugin list.
  • The shop, Nitro and quests explain why they do not work here instead of opening empty, and server discovery does the same for a bot.

Changes

  • Version numbers move up one notch at a time. Older versions were renumbered: the count stops at 1.0.9, then picks up at 1.1.1, 1.1.2, and so on.
  • The welcome message points to the Discord server rather than to an account.
  • The Documentation page is now the project's README, copied at every build, and knows how to show callouts, separators and links.

v1.1.5 — Grouped accounts everywhere

Fixes

  • The quick switch panel separates user accounts from bots too, like the "Manage accounts" window. Both lists read the same store, which now returns accounts already grouped.

v1.1.4 — Games and accounts

New

  • Games you are playing finally show on your profile. A list of detected processes was not enough: Nebula now publishes a real activity, the same path rich-presence clients take.
  • The account picker separates user accounts from bots under two headings, instead of stacking everything into one list.

Fixes

  • The "Discord App Detected" screen no longer replaces the client. It appeared because the client thought it was on the web; Nebula is the desktop application.

v1.1.3 — Identity and restraint

Fixes

  • The account limit is genuinely lifted. The first fix only touched the truncation that follows adding one; the refusal came from the button itself, which showed the error instead of opening the login. Both are fixed.
  • No trace of the original name left: repository, session id, download page, configuration editor and headers all carry Nebula now.

Lightening

  • Five Chromium services Nebula does not use are disabled: Cast discovery on the network, downloading optimisation rules, translation, autofill communication and content suggestions.
  • The disk cache is capped at 64 MB, where Chromium let it grow without limit.
  • Storage is written to disk once every five minutes instead of once a minute, with the moments that matter still covered.

v1.1.2 — More than five accounts

New

  • The picker accepts a hundred accounts instead of five. Discord was not refusing the sixth: its code cut the list at the fifth and signed the rest out. Neither a named constant nor a check function, the number was written into the slice itself, found by reading the modules the client loads.

v1.1.1 — Duplicate accounts

Fixes

  • An account signed in several times no longer appears twice in the list or in the menu. Each sign-in produces a different token, and entries were being compared by token rather than by account id.
  • The bot token field left the login window: it duplicated the one on the welcome screen.
  • The "user account" state is reset when Discord refuses the token or on sign-out, instead of staying active when nobody is signed in any more.

v1.0.9 — Token, restraint, updates

New

  • Signing into an account with a token, with no password and no two-factor code. The token is checked with Discord before being saved, so that nothing breaks later without explanation.

Lightening

  • No more request logging: every image and every script Discord loaded wrote a line to disk, which cost processor time constantly.
  • Game detection looks at processes once a minute instead of four times, and stops altogether when no window is on screen.

Fixes

  • No more update notifications from the original project. Nebula was querying its repository, and accepting one of its releases would have replaced Nebula with a different application.

v1.0.8 — Emoji, files and windows

New

  • The editor has an emoji catalogue: a button opens the list, grouped by server, with a search box. No more copying an id by hand, neither for text nor for buttons.
  • The editor accepts attachments: through the button or by drag and drop, ten files of 10 MB at most. A message can now contain nothing but a file.

Fixes

  • The title bar buttons act on their own window. Minimising or maximising from a second window drove the first, because the target window was deduced from a name rather than from where the click came.
  • The unavailable-page window takes the settings page's presentation.

v1.0.7 — Bots sign back in

Fixes

  • A bot could not sign in again after its first launch. Remembering it so it would show in the account list put its token, on restart, among the user account tokens — and those are sent to Discord as they are, without the prefix reserved for bots. Bots are now left out of that list.
  • The patches placed on Discord are isolated from one another: if one fails it is reported and the rest are still installed, instead of giving up on everything after it.

v1.0.6 — The editor really sends

Fixes

  • The message editor finally sends. The link with the client was properly established, but the page rejected the channel because it checked where the message came from: the channel is now handed over from the preload's isolated world, where that origin is not the page itself.
  • New windows no longer have the system's title bar: they take the main window's framing, where Discord draws its own.
  • The creator keeps his real badges. The decorative badges added to every profile no longer apply to his, which therefore shows only what he actually earned, plus Nebula's.
  • Bots are remembered as soon as they sign in, and appear in their category of the tray menu without having to go through the login window.
  • Creating or joining a server is intercepted earlier, when the window opens and no longer only on confirmation.

v1.0.5 — Nebula's message editor

New

  • A new message editor, written for Nebula, in place of the one borrowed elsewhere. It handles content, up to ten complete embeds (title, description, colour, author, thumbnail, large image, footer, date, aligned fields) and buttons that open a link, with custom emoji. The preview updates as you type and the "beta" label is gone.
  • The editor's button only appears on a bot, since it sends with the bot's credentials.
  • Creating or joining a server from a bot shows an explanation: a bot joins a server through its OAuth invite link, added by the server's owner.
  • Saved accounts are sorted into two categories, user accounts and bots, in the login window as in the tray menu. A bot opened in its own window is remembered for next time.

v1.0.4 — Every window in its place

Fixes

  • Signing into an account from a second window no longer hijacks the first. The login window remembers which one opened it and sends the account back there.
  • The unavailable page tells the truth case by case: on an account, that Nebula does not support this page and that the official client is needed; on a bot, that a bot has neither Nitro nor a subscription.
  • The Creator Nebula badge shows on a user account too. Its profile went straight to Discord, skipping the step that adds the badge.

v1.0.3 — Several windows and identity

New

  • The tray menu opens a new Discord window directly, and offers each saved account in its own window. Several Discords side by side, each with its own session: a bot here, your account there.
  • A Creator Nebula badge for the project's author, with the application's logo.
  • The welcome channel says who to contact if something goes wrong.

Fixes

  • Nitro, the shop and account connections are marked unavailable for everyone, not only for bots: they need Discord's payment and authentication machinery, which this client does not carry. That happens on the official client.
  • Detected games are written to the log the moment the client receives them, to tell a detection problem from a display problem.

v1.0.2 — Game detection and connections

New

  • Games you are playing are detected and shown in your activity. This client does not have Discord's native module, so Nebula reads the system's process list and compares it with the public catalogue of recognised games, every fifteen seconds.
  • A bot can open in its own window from the login window: an account in one window, a bot in another, at the same time.

Fixes

  • External account connections work again. The authentication callback arrives on a page with no token, and the list of routes forbidden to bots applied to that page as though it were an API call, which refused it every time. That list now only covers API calls.
  • Saved direct messages hold up better against an abrupt restart: storage is written to disk regularly, on sleep and on shutdown, instead of waiting for the browser's good will.
  • Settings open faster. The pages Nebula adds were rebuilt every time they opened, which forced the whole panel to remount.
  • A category no longer shows more than one "Unavailable" entry instead of stacking one per hidden page.

v1.0.1 — Fixes and finishing touches

Fixes

  • Settings no longer crash the client. The menu customisation applied to every nested panel instead of the root layout alone, which inserted sections where Discord expects none.
  • The system channel's profile finally shows with its badges, its bio and its banner. An internal route answered 404 before the profile was built, because route files are registered in alphabetical order and a catch-all came first.
  • The system channel stays at the top of the direct messages. Discord sorts by the last message's id, and its own was too low to rise.
  • The client starts in French even with a bot, which has no language of its own.

Interface

  • The system channel wears every Discord badge and its banner is animated.
  • Its creation date is Discord's own, 13 May 2015.
  • The application's logo is now round.
  • The tray menu has been slimmed down.
  • The plugin card shows Nebula as the publisher.
  • The Nebula section is a category in its own right, no longer an entry filed under Vencord.
  • The pages unavailable to a bot now cover connected applications and message permissions too.

Under the hood

  • The client's errors are written to the application's log, which makes it possible to diagnose a crash without opening the developer tools.
  • The pages Nebula adds are protected: an error in one of them shows in its place instead of taking the client down with it.

v1.0.0 — Nebula

Signing in with a Discord account

  • A new login window with email address and password.
  • Captcha handling in a window of its own, served from Discord's origin.
  • Two-factor authentication: app, SMS and backup codes.
  • Accounts are saved and reusable in one click.
  • Each account can open in its own window, with its own session.

User account compatibility

  • Direct messages, the member list, friends and the real profile are no longer replaced by the fake data meant for bots.
  • The email address and phone number shown are the real ones.
  • The automatic sign-out that targeted non-bot accounts has been removed.

Interface

  • The application is now called Nebula, with a new icon.
  • The whole application interface is in French.
  • A new Nebula section in the settings, with the application's information, the documentation and this page.
  • Pages reserved for user accounts show an explanation when a bot is signed in.
